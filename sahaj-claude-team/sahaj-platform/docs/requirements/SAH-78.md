## Problem
A clinic or hospital that wants to run a pharmacy and a store on Sahaj has nowhere to say what its stores are, who works in which store, which medicines and supplies it stocks, who it buys from, who must approve what, how documents are numbered, and what stock it starts with. Nothing in Phase 2 (Inventory) or Phase 3 (Pharmacy) can run until these masters exist: "Nothing else can run without these" (BRD section 1).

Today Sahaj has two separate drug lists that are not a stock master: `opd.medicine` (about 50 common medicines for OPD prescribing, SAH-7) and `ipd.drug` (14 medicines for IPD orders, SAH-31). Neither has strengths per pack, units, GST, vendors or stores. DEC-061 Q3 decided that Pharmacy & Inventory owns its own product master and that the two lists stay unchanged until Phase 3.

## Who uses it (roles) and in which setting (clinic, hospital ward, front desk)
Settings: the clinic or hospital office (setup), the central store, the pharmacy counter and wards (only for choosing a working store in this release).

Role names. The BRD uses eight P&I roles. Sahaj has eight team roles (owner, doctor, visiting doctor, nurse, front desk, billing, pharmacist, counsellor; `MembershipRole`, SAH-6). DEC-062 Q3 adds a ninth team role, **Store staff**, for store keepers and store assistants. A P&I role is the Sahaj team role plus a store role chosen per store. Decided mapping (OQ-78-6, OQ-78-7, OQ-78-10; DEC-062 Q3 A):

Who sees what in this release (contract 0.1.2, DEC-064, DEC-068 Q2 A): the owner reads and changes every master. A pharmacist (mapped or not) or a Central Store user, called "a reader of setup" in the stories, can read stores, products and vendors but cannot change them. Everyone else sees no "Inventory setup" tab; they still get the working-store switch if mapped to a store. The entry shows for the owner, any pharmacist, and anyone with at least one store mapping (ADR-028 D11, revised). Front desk, counsellor, visiting doctor and a Store staff member with no store mapping get no Pharmacy & Inventory access (403 "You do not have access to Pharmacy & Inventory. Ask your owner."); a Store staff member with no mapping lands on /app/pharmacy and sees that sentence. Store staff sees no other menu item, including HRA `staff`. A person with an entry but without SETUP_VIEW who opens Inventory setup sees "You do not have access to Inventory setup. Ask your owner." Wider read access for other mapped people is OQ-78-8: SETUP_VIEW, as described, is what is built.

## Business rules (numbered)
Scope, tenant, access
- BR-1. One Sahaj clinic account is one P&I unit (OQ-78-9, decided; ADR-028 D2: a `pharmacy.unit` row, one per tenant). The clinic comes from the signed-in user's session on every call, never from the request. Another clinic's data answers 404. The global product catalogue has no unit, is shared, and no unit can change it.
- BR-2. Only the owner creates, edits or switches off a master (stores, mappings, own products, adoption, local settings, vendors, approval rules, numbering, reasons, print templates, unit settings). Readers of setup, that is a pharmacist (team role, mapped to a store or not) or a Central Store user, may read stores, products and vendors, the staff table and the unit settings (SETUP_VIEW); the store list is a master read and shows every store of the unit, so a pharmacist sees store names, staff names, GSTIN and licence. Every call checks the role from the clinic membership on the server, not from the screen and not from the token alone.
- BR-3. The P&I entry and its API are available only when the clinic has the Pharmacy & Inventory module on. The switch is `pharmacy_on` in Plan and modules, Off by default, no trial, switched on by the Sahaj admin (OQ-78-10, decided). Off: the entry is hidden (not greyed) and the API answers 403 `module-off`.
- BR-4. Nothing in Masters is hard-deleted. Stores, mappings, own products, adoptions, vendors, reasons and numbering series are switched off (inactive) and then cannot be chosen for new work; old records keep their names. Un-adopting or switching off a product that has approved opening stock is refused until Phase 2 gives a way to clear stock (OQ-78-38, open).
- BR-5. Every create, change and switch-off writes an audit event (who, when, what, before and after values) through the platform audit. Masters hold no patient data, so audit text may include names of stores, products and vendors, but never a patient name, mobile number or ABHA number.
- BR-6. A person whose team access is switched off (SAH-6) loses P&I access at once in the same way (at most 60 seconds), and their store mappings stop counting. Their past records keep their name.

Unit settings
- BR-7. Unit settings hold: unit name (taken from the clinic profile, not re-typed), GSTIN, drug licence number and expiry, Integration mode (Integrated or Standalone), and the two fixed facts FEFO and negative stock blocked (BR-8). Integration mode is a single unit-level switch. Changing it deletes nothing and needs a confirmation that says what will be hidden or shown. The screens read what to show from one capability answer (`GET /context`, ADR-028 D5): integration mode, capabilities, unit, and the caller's stores and store roles. The screens show only what the capabilities list: hidden, not greyed. An Integrated-only action in Standalone mode is refused by the server (409 `integration-off`), not only hidden; no Phase 1 operation is Integrated-only, so this is tested when the first one arrives (SAH-79). Integrated shows OPD and IPD features of P&I when those modules are on; Standalone hides them. In Phase 1 every integrated capability answers "not used" (nothing to hide yet); only the switch and the capability answer are live and tested (this is the Phase 1 reading of BRD AC-M7, accepted with ADR-028). Every master works in both modes.
- BR-8. FEFO (first expiry, first out) is always on and negative stock is always blocked. They are shown as facts with no switch, and no API can change them (OQ-78-11, decided; ADR-028 OQ-PHI-8), because the BRD states "FEFO everywhere" and "stock never goes negative" as non-negotiable.

Stores and staff
- BR-9. A store has a name, a code, a type (Central Store, Pharmacy, Sub-store, Ward store), an issues-from store, "Sale to patients" Yes or No, "Consumption entry" Yes or No, an approver (HOD) and a delegate approver. Name and code are each unique in the unit, ignoring capital letters. A unit has exactly one active Central Store (OQ-78-12, decided); it is the only store that buys from vendors and it has no issues-from store. Every other store issues from the Central Store.
- BR-10. HOD and delegate are active team members mapped to that store. Choosing a HOD or delegate who is not yet mapped maps them with the matching store role. A delegate has a start date and an end date (OQ-78-15, DECIDED), lasts at most 90 calendar days counting both the first and the last day (90 allowed, 91 refused with "A delegation can be at most 90 days"; DEC-064 R2-9, DEC-068 Q1 A), is a different person from the HOD, and one delegate is active per store at a time. The owner may choose the HOD and delegate when creating the very first Central Store (F-11). After the end date the delegate no longer approves; the history shows the dates. A store with no active HOD shows "No approver set" and, when the chosen approval rules need an HOD, a warning.
- BR-11. Access is given per store, not per user. A person can be mapped to many stores with a store role in each (Store user, Central Store user, Approver, Delegate), once per store. There is no Pharmacist store role. What a person may do (raise indent, approve, receive, consume or return) is worked out by the server from the store role, the store's flags and the person's Sahaj role, and is shown read-only in the mapping table (mockup 16 columns). The pharmacist-only actions, and the display "incl. Schedule X" for receive, follow the team role PHARMACIST only; the server checks this on every call. A mapping whose Approver or Delegate role no longer fits the person's team role "needs review" and counts as Store user. The owner has every store capability except pharmacist-only actions. Delegate approval powers show only while the delegate dates are current. The Store staff team role may be mapped only as Store user or Central Store user.
- BR-12. Every store-bound call names the store in its path (`{storeId}`); the server never takes a "current store" from the client and never from the request body. The server answers: another clinic's store, 404; a store of this clinic to which the caller is not mapped, 404 (it does not confirm that the store exists; the owner is never "unmapped"); mapped but missing the capability, 403 `store-permission-denied` (audited); bad input, 400. The store check runs before any filter, sort or paging. A person mapped to several stores picks a working store after sign-in (and may switch at any time); the screen keeps the choice and the server remembers the last one per person for the next sign-in, for convenience only. Order of answers (contract general rule 2, code review N-9): module off 403, access level 403, invalid path id 400, then a body that cannot be read 400 (right after the id check, before the 404; it reveals nothing about whether the store exists), then 404 or the store guard, then field checks 400 (after the 404), then 409, then 422. Exception (DEC-077): once the line-send cap of an opening stock file is reached, a request with a bad body answers 409, not 400, because the state check comes first; the earlier order is otherwise unchanged.

Product master
- BR-13. The product master has two sources: the global catalogue (read-only, shared by all units, code `G-nnnnnn`) and the unit's own products (code `U-<unit code>-nnnn`). A unit adopts a global product and sets only local fields; global fields (name, salt, strength, form, manufacturer, HSN, GST %, schedule, units, conversion) stay read-only for the unit.
- BR-14. Before an own product is saved, the server looks for a global product (first) and then an own product with the same salt, strength and form (OQ-78-17, decided) and, if found, the screen offers it ("Use global product" or "Create anyway"). The choice is audited. Creating anyway is allowed (mockup 16).
- BR-15. Every product has a purchase unit, an issue unit and a conversion: how many issue units are in one purchase unit (a whole number, 1 or more; "Box of 10 → vial" is 10; "Kit → kit" is 1). All transactions after receiving stock use the issue unit; quantities are whole issue units. Once a product has any approved opening stock (and later any stock movement) its units and conversion are locked, because changing them would silently change every quantity.
- BR-16. An own product needs: name, salt or generic name, strength, form, purchase unit, issue unit, conversion, HSN, GST %, and an explicit schedule choice (None, H, H1, X) with no default. Manufacturer is required unless the owner marks the product "made in-house". Values for HSN, GST % and schedule are the owner's responsibility and are never defaulted by Sahaj (OQ-78-19, decided; go-live item G-2).
- BR-17. Local settings per adopted or own product: reorder level per store (issue units, whole number 0 or more), rack per store (up to 30 characters), preferred vendor (an active vendor of the unit), alias (what staff call it), barcode (unique among the unit's active products), returnable Yes or No. The BRD's "MRP / selling rule" is not built (OQ-78-20, decided).
- BR-18. Search in the product master looks at global and own products together, ignores capital letters, and matches the barcode exactly (ignoring capital letters) while name, salt, alias and code match as a part of the text. An inactive product is not offered for new work.
- BR-19. No global product exists for a unit until it adopts it; adoption is per unit and can be repeated safely (adopting twice changes nothing).
- BR-33. The global catalogue in this release is about 30 sample products, seeded for development, test and demo only, each marked "Sample · not clinically validated", with made-up manufacturer names and sample HSN, GST and schedule values. It is never loaded in production. A real catalogue is a separate later story, after the human names a licensed source and a pharmacist reviews it (OQ-78-16, decided; go-live item G-10).

Vendors
- BR-20. A vendor has name, GSTIN, drug licence number and expiry (or "not required", for consumables), contact, credit days, return policy (days before expiry, credit %) and the stores it supplies to. Vendor name is unique in the unit, ignoring capital letters. A switched-off vendor can be switched on again by the owner (DEC-078 item 7); the name stays unique, so without this a switched-off vendor could not be re-added. A vendor also has two optional credit levels, "Credit for goods damaged on receipt" and "Credit after expiry", each 0 to 100 with at most 2 decimals; an empty level means the standard credit applies. They are built in SAH-78 (contract 0.2.1, migration 1290) and are used by the vendor return of SAH-79 (OQ-79-11; a line both damaged on receipt and expired starts from the lower of the two levels, DEC-078 item 5 amended, G-18); this replaces the earlier plan to add them with the SAH-79 WP-E migration (DEC-078 item 5).
- BR-21. A vendor whose drug licence has expired is shown "Blocked · licence expired" and must not get a new purchase order (AC-M5 of the BRD; enforced when POs exist in SAH-79, and exposed now as a status the PO story must read). The licence is valid through its expiry date and blocks from the next day, with no grace days. A vendor marked "not required" is never blocked for licence. Existing documents of a blocked vendor are untouched. The licence number is free text up to 40 characters; Sahaj does not verify it with any authority (OQ-78-23, decided; go-live item G-3).

Approval rules
- BR-22. Approval rules are data, not code. For each transaction type an owner sets: number of levels (0, 1 or 2) and the role of each level; an optional condition (store type, amount in ₹, item flag Schedule X or high value, priority); ₹ limits (PO, write-off, discount, sales return, stock adjustment); escalation after N hours; and the delegate rule (a delegate, set per store in BR-10). Changing a rule applies to new transactions only and never to ones already waiting. The transaction type list is OQ-78-25 (open). (DEC-078 items 1 to 3 and 9.) The limit of an amount condition is at most ₹ 9,99,99,999. The rate-rise rule (`GRN_RATE_RISE`) takes a percentage only (Always, or Above a percentage), like the discount. The server says, for each preset, which transaction types need a limit, and the client does not guess. The role matrix of mockup 05 (a read-out of what the engine enforces, DEC-074 OQ-80-38) is not built in this release; it can be added with SAH-80.
- BR-23. Three presets ship: Lean (no HOD step), Standard (one level, HOD), Strict (HOD, then owner above a limit). Applying a preset fills the rules and can be changed afterwards. Sahaj supplies no ₹ amounts: a preset that needs a limit asks the owner for it (OQ-78-26 and OQ-78-28, decided; go-live items G-4, G-5). Escalation defaults are stored only: indent 2 hours (urgent 30 minutes), transfer 24 hours (OQ-78-29, decided).
- BR-24. Three rules are fixed and shown but cannot be switched off by any setting: nobody approves their own request; Schedule X needs a pharmacist and a second signature (who the second signer is: left to SAH-80, OQ-78-30, decided; go-live item G-6); the stock ledger cannot be edited (corrections are reversing lines). Phase 1 shows and stores nothing for them beyond displaying them; Phase 2 and 3 enforce them. Approval of opening stock by an owner is also fixed and is not an approval rule (ADR-028 D7).

Numbering, reasons, templates
- BR-25. Each document type (PO, GRN, indent, transfer, bill) has one numbering series per unit: prefix, sequence length, and whether the sequence restarts at the start of the financial year (1 April). The counter is kept per unit, document type and financial year (April to March, IST). Numbers are unique, never reused and never edited; for documents that are saved there are no gaps within a financial year, and a cancelled document keeps its number. Once any number of the current financial year has been issued, the series cannot be changed (409 `series-locked` "Numbers have already been issued this year."); the lock follows the financial year, which starts on 1 April IST. While no number has been issued in the current financial year, the prefix and format can be changed, so a new prefix starts at the first number of a new financial year (DEC-078 item 4; this replaces the earlier reading of ADR-028 D8 "only the prefix, and only from the next financial year"). A format change applies to new numbers only. A bill number is at most 16 characters (OQ-78-31, decided; go-live item G-7). There is no separate bill series per store in this release.
- BR-26. Reason lists exist for store return, sales return, damage and stock adjustment (OQ-78-32, open), each seeded from the mockup and shown to users as chips. With SAH-79 there are 11 lists: Receipt rejection, Central Store change, Approval rejection, Stock transfer, Consumption purpose, Dispute, Vendor return, and the four SAH-78 lists (DEC-075 item 7, DEC-078 item 6). The owner can add a reason and switch one off; a switched-off reason stays on old records. Seeded reasons cannot be renamed or deleted. The fixed reason (`receipt-reject.short`) cannot be switched off, and the server says which reasons are fixed (a `fixed` flag); the client does not recognise it by key.
- BR-27. Print templates exist for invoice, issue slip and debit note. The owner sets header lines, footer note and paper size; legally required fields (such as GSTIN, drug licence, HSN, batch, expiry on an invoice) cannot be hidden (OQ-78-33, decided; go-live item G-8). Printing uses the browser print view.

Opening stock
- BR-28. Opening stock is loaded from a CSV file in a Sahaj template with the columns store, product, batch, expiry, MRP, purchase rate and quantity (BRD M-section "Opening stock"). The server checks every row and shows all problems before anything is kept. A file with any error is not imported; nothing is partly imported. The file itself is not stored. Two rows for the same store, product and batch are an error; batch numbers are compared ignoring capital letters ("AB1" and "ab1" are the same batch), and store codes and product codes in a row match ignoring capital letters and leading or trailing spaces.
- BR-29. An import is not final until the owner approves it, and the owner who approves must be a different person from the one who uploaded it (the fixed rule "nobody approves their own request"). Store staff (Central Store user or pharmacist mapped to the store) normally upload and the owner approves; if an owner uploads, a different owner must approve (OQ-78-36, decided; go-live item G-9). An approved import cannot be edited or cancelled; corrections are made in Phase 2 with stock adjustments. Opening stock is allowed once per store (OQ-78-37, open). The file rules are in OQ-78-35 (open).
- BR-30. Opening stock posts as an "opening stock" line in the stock ledger. SAH-78 does upload, checks, review and owner approval and keeps the approved lines as "approved opening stock". The ledger is created in Phase 2 and the posting is the first SAH-79 story (OQ-78-34, decided; ADR-028 OQ-PHI-9). Until posted, no screen shows the quantity as usable stock.
- BR-34. (Decided by DEC-071 Q1 A, 18 Nov 2026, auto while the human was away; the human confirms at the merge ask.) While an opening stock file is open, that is a draft (rows still being sent) or waiting for approval, three changes are refused with 409 `open-import` and the sentence "Finish or reject the opening stock file of this store first.": (a) switching its store off or changing the store's code; (b) switching a product on one of its lines off; (c) changing the units or conversion of a product on one of its lines. The block ends when the file is approved, rejected or replaced; an approved file then has its own locks (BR-15, BR-9, OQ-78-38). Reason: without it the store could be switched off and the file could no longer be approved or rejected, and approval would rest on units that changed after the owner checked the file (code review N-2). Other fields of the store and product can still be changed. Contract change: one new problem code on existing 409 answers (additive, pharmacy.yaml 0.1.3, architect).
- BR-35. (Decided by DEC-076 and DEC-077, 18 Nov 2026, auto.) Closing and limiting an opening stock file:
  - An owner who may approve at the store may reject a file that is a DRAFT as well as one that is waiting for approval, with a reason from the list. The uploader can still replace their own draft. A rejected file frees the store and its products at once (settles OQ-78-42).
  - The cap on line sends counts changed rows written, at most twice the row count per file. A request whose rows are all unchanged writes nothing and does not count.
  - A person can start at most 10 new files per store per day (IST). The 11th answers 409 "Too many files today. Try again tomorrow."
  - `start`, `submit` and `approve` refuse (404) a store that has been switched off, and a store switched off in the meantime cannot get a new file.

Formats and existing Sahaj
- BR-31. UI text is plain English in sentence case. Money is ₹ with Indian grouping, with paise shown when an amount has them (₹ 1,25,000 or ₹ 1,25,000.50); amounts are held to the paisa. Dates are 24 Oct 2026; times 11:05 AM IST. The mockup's "till Mar 2028" for a licence expiry is shown as the full date entered, "till 31 Mar 2028" (deviation from the mockup text, for sign-off with the go-live item G-3).
- BR-32. Additive only (DEC-061 Answer 2). With the module switch Off (the default), the sidebar, every menu, every existing screen, every existing API and its JSON, and every existing test behave exactly as before. Existing roles keep their meaning. The Store staff option appears on the Team and access screen only while the module is On; `POST /team` accepts STORE_STAFF while the module is Off (tech lead decision, 18 Nov 2026; settles OQ-78-40(b)). Store staff sees no other menu item, including HRA `staff` (DEC-068 Q2 A).

## Patient-safety and privacy points
- No patient data is entered or stored in Masters. No patient name, mobile or ABHA number may appear in a master, a log or an audit value. Vendor and staff contacts are business data and are never logged.
- The product master decides what the pharmacy may later sell and dispense, so its safety fields matter: schedule (H, H1, X), the issue unit and conversion, and the duplicate check. A wrong schedule flag could let a Schedule X or H1 drug be sold without its register or second signature in Phase 3; a wrong conversion makes every quantity wrong. Hence: schedule has no default and must be chosen (BR-16); global fields are read-only (BR-13); units and conversion lock once stock exists (BR-15); duplicates are warned and the choice is audited (BR-14).
- The sample catalogue is not clinically validated (BR-33): it must never be used for real patients or real stock.
- Duplicate products split stock and break first-expiry-first-out. The duplicate check and the unique barcode rule reduce that.
- Negative stock and non-FEFO selling are not switchable (BR-8).
- The fixed rules (no self-approval, Schedule X two signatures, append-only ledger) cannot be turned off by any setting (BR-24).
- Expired vendor licences block new orders (BR-21): buying medicines from an unlicensed supplier is a legal and safety risk. The licence number and validity are the owner's entry; Sahaj does not verify them with any authority.
- Access is per store and checked on the server on every call (BR-11, BR-12): a user cannot see or act on a store they are not mapped to by changing the screen or the request. The pharmacist-only actions stay with pharmacists, also against the owner.
- Seed and test data for the global catalogue, vendors and licences is sample data and clearly marked; no real licence numbers, GSTINs or patient data (BR-33).
