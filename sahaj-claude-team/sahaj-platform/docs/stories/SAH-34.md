Roles in this file: OWNER = owner, DOCTOR = doctor, NURSE = nurse, FRONT_DESK = front desk, BILLING = billing, PHARMACIST = pharmacist, VISITING_DOCTOR = visiting doctor. "Scoped" means a user whose scope is narrower than all (in phase 1 only a NURSE with the clinic switch on).

## 1. Stories

### SAH-34B · As the owner of a hospital, I want a nurse to reach only the patients of her own wards on every IPD screen and call, and to be told plainly when something is outside her view, so that patient records go only to staff who care for the patient and no one mistakes a partial board for the whole hospital
Priority: Must · Size L

### SAH-34C · As a nurse (or any scoped user), I want the Admitted list to show which patients are mine and how many of the clinic's patients I can see, so that I know what the list does and does not show
Priority: Must · Size S

### SAH-34E · As a nurse, I want to open one patient outside my wards for 30 minutes with a stated reason, so that I can help a patient who needs me now, and the access is recorded in my name
Priority: Must · Size M

### SAH-34F · As the owner, I want every emergency access listed until I have reviewed it, so that no access outside the normal scope goes unchecked, even if the patient has gone home
Priority: Must · Size S

## Acceptance criteria
Numbering runs through the whole file so that tests and the requirement can point to one number. "Rule" numbers are those of `docs/requirements/SAH-34.md`.

### SAH-34B · Scope enforcement (server)

**Scope and the switch**
1. Given any IPD request in a clinic whose IPD module is Trial, On or Read-only, when it arrives, then the server builds the caller's scope once, after the membership re-read, and applies it as: OWNER, DOCTOR, FRONT_DESK, BILLING and PHARMACIST see all admitted patients on the endpoints their role already has (unchanged); NURSE sees all while the clinic switch "Nurses see only their wards" is off and only her wards when it is on; VISITING_DOCTOR is scoped to patients whose admitting doctor is her membership (the rule exists and is unit-tested) but keeps 403 `forbidden` on every IPD endpoint until story 34D. The scope comes only from the signed-in user, the clinic setting and the data on the server: no request field, header or query can choose or widen it. (Rule 1, 5)
2. Given the switch is off for a clinic (the state of every clinic at deploy), when any role uses any IPD screen or call, then the answers are identical to those before this story, including for NURSE (regression: the existing IPD suites pass unchanged as NURSE). (Rule 2)
3. Given the switch is on and a NURSE has one or more live ward assignments, when she lists or opens patients, then she sees an admitted patient if and only if the patient's current bed is in one of those wards. Given two nurses assigned to the same ward, then both see it. Given a nurse assigned to two wards, then she sees both. Given an assignment that belongs to an inactive membership, then it counts for nothing. (Rule 3)
4. Given a patient is moved to a bed in another ward, when the nurse's next request arrives, then the patient is gone from her lists and refused on details and writes; the nurses of the new ward see the patient at their next request. There is no grace period. (Rule 3; handover gap: OQ-34-2)
5. Given the switch is on and a NURSE has no live ward assignment, when she lists, then the list is empty; when she opens a patient, writes, or calls a by-id route, then the answer is 403 `outside-scope`; the Safety board holds no patient. The server never treats "no assignment" as "all wards". (Rule 4)
6. Given the scope cannot be built (the assignment read fails, the clinic setting cannot be read, the membership is missing or inactive), when the request arrives, then it fails (an error answer, not data), nothing is returned or written, and a WARN log line with tenant id and request id (no names) is written. Given the clinic has no stored setting row, then the switch is read as off (the deploy default). (Rule 4)
7. Given a change of the nurse's role, her ward assignment, her membership state or the clinic switch, when her next request arrives, then it applies at once (the role and membership are read from the database on every request, not only from the token). (Rule 5)

**Lists and reads**
8. Given `GET /admissions`, when a scoped user calls it, then only in-scope rows are returned; each row says whether it is "mine" (the caller is the admitting doctor; for a NURSE see OQ-34-9) without sending a membership id; the answer carries the visible count N and the total admitted count M of the clinic as numbers only; the list-level abilities and the role-gated fields stay as SAH-36 (every listed row is in scope, so one set of abilities is still right); FRONT_DESK still gets no clinical data. The wards filter lists every ward (OQ-34-22). (Rule 5, 9)
9. Given `GET /board` (bed board), when a scoped user calls it, then the status of every bed stays, and the patient name and details of an occupied bed outside her scope are absent from the answer (not merely hidden by the screen). (Rule 8; OQ-34-6)
10. Given the detail reads `GET /admissions/{id}`, `/nursing`, `/emr`, `/mar`, `/discharge`, `/deposits` and `/charges` (read), when the admission is outside the caller's scope, then the answer is 403 `outside-scope` with no patient data in the body (no name, bed, ward, UHID); when it is in scope, the answer and the record-access rows are exactly as today. (Rule 5, 6)
11. Given `GET /clinical-events`, then OWNER and DOCTOR see all entries (unchanged); NURSE sees her own entries and the entries of admissions in her scope (SAH-33 AC16, which becomes true), including entries of an admission she can see only through an emergency access. (Rule 7)

**Writes and by-id routes**
12. Given the writes `POST /admissions/{id}/vitals`, `/medicine-orders`, `/transfer`, `/discharge`, `/discharge/signoff`, `/deposits`, `/charges`, `POST /medicine-orders/{id}/stop` and `/medicine-orders/{id}/doses`, when the admission is outside the caller's scope, then the answer is 403 `outside-scope` and nothing is written (no row, no clinical event, no audit event of care). The check is made inside the transaction after the admission row is locked, so a transfer, a discharge or a grant expiry racing with the write is decided on locked data. (Rule 5)
13. Given a route that carries an order id or an escalation id (`/medicine-orders/{id}/stop`, `/doses`, `/escalations/{id}/acknowledge`, `/respond`, `/call-senior`), when it is called, then the server finds the admission of that order or escalation and applies the same guard; an id of another clinic is 404 (RLS), never `outside-scope`. (Rule 5, 15)
14. **(Reworded 10 Nov 2026 after review; was "IPD Off first, then role".)** Given a request, then the answers keep this order: first the role check (403 `forbidden`) and the IPD Off check (403 `module-off`), in the endpoint's existing order of these two; then id not found or another clinic 404; then out of scope 403 `outside-scope`. Most IPD endpoints check the role first, and so do the Emergency access picker and Open; a few older ones (for example `GET /admissions/{id}/discharge` and `PUT /admissions/{id}/discharge/signoff`) check module Off first, and each keeps its order. Both refusals are 403 answers with fixed text and no patient data, so which comes first reveals nothing. What is guaranteed and testable: (a) a missing or other-clinic id is 404 and never `outside-scope`; (b) `outside-scope` is answered only to a role that may use the endpoint, and only with the module not Off; (c) a refused request of either kind writes nothing. (Rule 6; ADR-017 D3; design section 1.2)

**The Safety board and the limit is never silent**
15. Given `GET /safety` and `GET /safety/summary` for a scoped NURSE, then escalations, the red notice, the Medicine round (doses due or overdue), the tiles, the tab number and her part of the audit trail are computed on her scope (own entries and in-scope admissions) and agree with each other: tab number = tile = rows not closed, and the dose counts follow the SAH-35 invariants, per caller. (Rule 7a)
16. Given the tile "Your patients", then N is the caller's visible count; the small text reads "In your wards" when the caller's scope is wards, and "All admitted patients" when the switch is off (SAH-33 AC4 wording). (Rule 7a)
17. Given `POST /escalations/{id}/acknowledge`, `/respond` and `/call-senior`, when the escalation belongs to a patient outside the caller's scope, then the answer is 403 `outside-scope` (a nurse cannot Call senior for a patient she cannot see; the route to act is Emergency access with the reason "Patient deteriorating, rapid response"). OWNER and DOCTOR are unaffected. (Rule 7e; OQ-34-23)
18. Given OWNER and DOCTOR, then `GET /safety`, `/safety/summary`, the banner data and the audit trail are never narrowed by this story: an escalation of a patient who is in no nurse's scope (for example a ward with no nurse assigned) still appears for them with the same counts as before. A test proves it. (Rule 7b)
19. Given a scoped NURSE's Safety answer, then it carries the scope sentence data (N, M, and whether she has any ward) so that the board can say it is scoped; the wording is in story 34C (AC 37). The answer contains no name or detail of any patient outside her scope. (Rule 7c)
20. Given a scoped NURSE and one or more Open escalations of patients outside her scope that are past their response target (SAH-33 AC7 rule, database clock), then her Safety answer also carries that count as a number, with no name, bed, ward, score or id; the count is not part of the tab number, the tile or the rows (AC 15 stays true). With none past target the field is 0. For OWNER, DOCTOR and an unscoped NURSE it is absent. **A BA safety addition, not in the mockup or the ADR; needs the human and the clinical sign-off (OQ-34-26).** (Rule 7d)

**What is not scoped, coverage, privacy**
21. Given the Emergency queue and cases, the Patients (OPD) search and file, the pharmacist check list and its verify and reject (OWNER and PHARMACIST only), the configuration endpoints (wards, card setup, settings) and admitting a patient, then they are unchanged by this story. Given the second-nurse witness check of a high-alert dose (SAH-38), then the witness need not be in scope and the check reads no patient data (OQ-34-25). (Rule 8)
22. Given the IPD contract, then an automated test lists every IPD endpoint and fails when an endpoint that lists admitted patients or takes an admission, order or escalation id has neither the scope guard nor an entry on the written exemption list of AC 21, so that a later endpoint cannot forget the guard. (Rule 5)
23. Given reads in scope, then they write the same record-access rows as today; the list writes none (DEC-021); a refused request writes none and no `deny` clinical event (OQ-34-24). Given the privacy register, then DEC-021's go-live condition "revisit when ward scoping arrives" is answered by: with the switch on, the list shows only the user's wards. (Rule 14, 15)
24. Given logs and audit, then no patient name, mobile number, ABHA number, UHID or clinical text is written to an application log by the guard; a refusal logs tenant id, request id, route name, the caller's user id and, if known, the admission id. (Rule 14)
25. Given another clinic, then its assignments, switch, admissions and grants are never read for this caller (the clinic comes from the signed-in user; RLS). (Rule 15)

Notes for design
- One scope object built once per request after the membership re-read (ADR-013 D3): ALL, WARDS(set), OWN(admitting doctor), NONE, plus the admissions of the caller's active emergency grants (story 34E). Lists add one predicate to the set-based reads; details and writes call the guard; writes call it after the admission lock (ADR-006 D4). The architect names the problem type `outside-scope` in the contract. No database change is made by this story beyond what 34A and 34E bring.
- 34B reads the ward-assignment table and the switch of story 34A. Until 34A's screen exists no owner can switch the scope on; tests create assignments directly (OQ-34-35).
- For AC 20 the count is an aggregate over the clinic computed with the caller's scope subtracted; it must not be built by loading out-of-scope rows into the response.

### SAH-34C · Admitted list: the scope chip and "My patients"

26. Given the Admitted tab and a scoped user (a NURSE with the switch on), then the toolbar shows the chip with the shield icon: "Patients in their wards · N of M" (first letter capital; N visible, M admitted in the clinic, both from the server), and the full sentence as its tooltip and accessible name: "As Nurse you see patients in their wards (N of M)." The chip sits with the Emergency access button (story 34E) as in the mockup's final layout. (Rule 9; changed sentence: OQ-34-27)
27. Given a user whose scope is all (OWNER, DOCTOR, FRONT_DESK, a NURSE with the switch off), then there is no chip and no notice (as the mockup: shown only when the scope is not "all"). Given nobody is admitted, then the chip is not shown and "No one is admitted right now." shows as today. (Rule 9)
28. Given the mockup's sentence ends "The owner sets this in Clinic setup → Roles and access.", then that panel is not built (C1), so the sentence is not shown; the tooltip ends with "The owner sets your wards in Clinic setup." instead, to be finalised with the screen of 34A. (Rule 9; OQ-34-27)
29. Given a NURSE with the switch on and no live ward, then the chip reads "Patients in their wards · 0 of M" and the list area shows, in place of cards and of "No patients match these filters.": "No wards are assigned to you, so no patients are shown. Ask the owner to assign your ward. If a patient needs you now, use Emergency access." (new text). The Emergency access button is available. (Rule 4, 9, 17; OQ-34-27)
30. Given a NURSE with wards but no patient in them, while others are admitted, then the list area shows "No patients in your wards right now." (new text), and the chip reads "… · 0 of M". Given filters or search that match nobody, the text stays "No patients match these filters." (SAH-36). (Rule 9)
31. Given a scoped user, then the number on the "Admitted" tab is N (the patients she can see), not M, so that tab number, chip and rows agree (OQ-34-28). (Rule 7a)
32. Given the toolbar, then the button "My patients" (user icon, `aria-pressed`) is shown to OWNER, DOCTOR and, for a NURSE, only when she has at least one live ward assignment; it is not shown to FRONT_DESK (as the mockup). BILLING, PHARMACIST and VISITING_DOCTOR have no list. (Rule 9; mockup deviation for NURSE, OQ-34-9)
33. Given "My patients" is pressed, then the list narrows to rows the server marked "mine": for OWNER and DOCTOR the admitting doctor is the caller; for a NURSE the patient is in one of her wards. It is a filter, not a security rule: the server does not rely on it. It applies to Cards and Table, combines with search and ward (all must match), is kept in the page's memory only (not stored, not sent), and shows "No patients match these filters." when empty. (Rule 9)
34. Given the Ward select, then it lists every ward of the clinic ("All wards" first) for every user (OQ-34-22). Given a scoped nurse picks a ward that has none of her patients, then the list shows "No patients match these filters.". (Rule 9)
35. Given a search for a name, UHID or bed, then it runs on the loaded, already-scoped list on the device; a patient outside the scope is never found and the screen gives no hint that one exists (it says "No patients match these filters."). The search text is not sent, logged or stored (SAH-36 AC19). (Rule 5, 15)
36. Given the red banner "Open safety board" (SAH-36 kept it), the Safety tab number and the Table view, then all follow the same scoped set as the cards (AC 15). (Rule 7a)
37. Given a NURSE on the Safety tab, then above the tiles a note reads "This board shows your wards only (N of M patients)." (new text), and for a nurse with no ward "No wards are assigned to you, so this board shows no patients."; a scoped nurse's "All within target" and "No escalations…" therefore never stand alone. Given the count of AC 20 is above 0, then a red notice reads "N escalation(s) past the response target in other wards. Tell the doctor now, or use Emergency access if you must act." with no names. Given OWNER and DOCTOR, neither text is shown. (Rule 7c, 7d; OQ-34-26, OQ-34-27)
38. Given a sheet or page that reads one patient (admission, Nursing, EMR, MAR, discharge, deposits) and the server answers 403 `outside-scope` (after a transfer, or after an emergency access ended), then the sheet closes, that patient's clinical data is cleared from the screen and from the page's memory (the pattern of SAH-40 and G-2), the message "This patient is not in your wards." shows (for a patient the user had through an emergency access: "Your emergency access to this patient has ended."), and the list reloads and drops the patient. The whole Admitted page is not locked (this is not `forbidden` or `module-off`). (Rule 6; OQ-34-11, OQ-34-15)
39. Given a save from a sheet (vitals, a dose, an order, a transfer, a deposit, a discharge step) answered 403 `outside-scope`, then nothing is shown as saved, the sheet closes with the message of AC 38 plus "Nothing was saved.", and the user can open Emergency access if she still needs the patient. Whether the typed values stay on screen is OQ-34-15 (default: they do not). (Rule 5, 6; **SAFETY**, OQ-34-15)
40. Given the 60-second reload (SAH-36), then the chip, N and M, "mine" and the tab number follow the server; a patient who leaves or enters the scope shows or disappears at the next reload; the search, ward, view and "My patients" choices are kept. Given a failed reload, then the last data stays with "Could not refresh. Showing data from <time>." and N, M are not recomputed on the device. (Rule 9)
41. Given a slow network, then the existing "Loading beds and admissions" shows; given a failed first load, the existing "We could not load beds and admissions. Try again." with **Try again**. The chip is never drawn from a guess: before the first answer it is absent. (Rule 9)
42. Given a keyboard or screen-reader user, then the chip's tooltip text is also its accessible name, "My patients" announces its pressed state, the zero-ward text is a polite status message, and every button is at least 44 px high on a tablet. (Rule 9)
43. Given privacy, then the browser stores nothing about scope (no local storage, no URL, no console output of names or counts), and a user who signs out and in as another user never sees the previous user's chip or list. (Rule 15)

Notes for design
- Mostly a screen: chip, toggle, texts, refusal handling. N, M, "mine" and whether the nurse has a ward come from the server (`GET /admissions`); the browser does no scoping and no counting of the clinic.
- "N of M" shows the hospital census to every scoped user (low sensitivity, as the mockup).
- AC 37 is shown on the Safety tab, so it is built here with the Safety tab component; the server data is AC 19 and AC 20.

### SAH-34E · Break-glass emergency access

**Entry and sheet**
44. Given the Admitted tab and a scoped user, then the chip carries a small ghost button **Emergency access** (key icon). Given a user whose scope is all (OWNER, DOCTOR, FRONT_DESK, a NURSE with the switch off), then there is no button and the server refuses a grant request from her. Given BILLING, PHARMACIST and VISITING_DOCTOR, then they have no list and no button. (Rule 10)
45. Given the button, then it opens the sheet **Emergency access** with the subtitle "For a patient outside your wards. Access lasts 30 minutes, is logged, and the owner reviews it."; a select **Patient** listing every admitted patient outside her scope as "<name> · bed <code> · <UHID>"; a select **Reason** with the placeholder "Choose a reason" and the options "Patient deteriorating, rapid response", "Covering for a colleague on break", "Asked to help by the duty doctor"; a checkbox "I understand this access is recorded with my name and reviewed by the owner"; and the buttons **Cancel** and **Open for 30 minutes** (key icon). (Rule 10; UHID in place of the mockup's MRN, OQ-36-8)
46. Given nothing is outside her scope (she has every ward, or nobody else is admitted), then the sheet shows "You can already see every admitted patient." and only **Cancel**. (Rule 10)
47. Given **Open for 30 minutes** with no reason chosen, then the sheet stays open and shows "Choose a reason for emergency access."; given the box not ticked, "Tick the box to confirm this access is recorded."; nothing is sent. Given a request straight to the server without a reason from the list, or without `confirmed: true`, then the server answers 400 and stores nothing. (Rule 10)
48. Given the server, then it accepts a grant only from a caller whose scope is narrower than all and whose role may use the endpoint; FRONT_DESK, BILLING, PHARMACIST, OWNER and DOCTOR get 403 `forbidden`; VISITING_DOCTOR keeps 403 until 34D. (Rule 10)
49. Given the patient, then the server accepts only an admitted patient of the caller's clinic who is outside her scope; a discharged, unknown or other-clinic admission is 404; a patient already in her scope (own ward, or an active grant of hers) is refused with a clear conflict answer and no new row. (Rule 10, 11; OQ-34-14)

**The grant**
50. Given a valid request, then the server creates one grant for this caller and this admission with the reason key; the start time and the end time (start + 30 minutes) are set by the database clock, never by the browser; the duration cannot be sent or changed. (Rule 11)
51. Given the grant is made, then the sheet closes, the list search is cleared (as the mockup), the patient joins the caller's list with the grey flag **Emergency access** (key icon) on its card, and the toast reads "Emergency access to <patient name> for 30 minutes. The owner will see this on the Safety board." The toast shows only after the server has answered. The flag is shown only to the person who opened the grant; its tooltip may state the end time (OQ-34-33). (Rule 11, 12)
52. Given an active grant, then the caller can do on that one admission what her role can do (a NURSE: Nursing, MAR, vitals, as SAH-36 abilities) and nothing more; no other patient, no other role's action and no other module (the Patients search) is opened by it. The patient's safety flags (Not seen, EWS, Allergy) show on the card as for any in-scope patient. (Rule 11)
53. Given a grant, then it is checked on every request against the database clock: after the end time the next request for that patient is refused 403 `outside-scope`, the list drops the patient at its next reload, and the screen follows AC 38 and 39. A sheet left open past minute 30 saves nothing. (Rule 11)
54. Given a grant, then there is no extension and no "End now". After expiry the user opens a new grant (new reason, new review row). Given a second request for a patient for whom the same user has an active grant, then no new row is written and the expiry is unchanged. Given a double tap, the button is disabled while the request runs and one grant results. (Rule 11)
55. Given an active grant and the patient is discharged, or the grantee's membership becomes inactive, or her role changes, then it stops giving access from the next request (the grant is bound to her membership and role) and it stays in the owner's list (story 34F). (Rule 11, 13)
56. Given a second user opens a grant on the same patient, then the first user's grant stays as its own row and its own review line; a grant of one user never shows a flag or access to another user. (Rule 11)

**Picker, records, plan state**
57. Given the picker, then the answer holds, for each out-of-scope admitted patient, the name, bed code, UHID and the admission reference only: no diagnosis, allergy, vitals, ward notes or bill; it is given only to a caller allowed to open a grant (AC 48); it writes no record-access row; it is not logged. It is a directory of admitted patients before a reason is given (the mockup's choice; option P1); this is listed for the security review and the privacy register. (Rule 15; OQ-34-11)
58. Given a grant is made, then in the same transaction: (1) a clinical audit entry of kind `bg` is written with the fixed text "Emergency access opened · <reason text from the list>", the grantee as user and the admission set; it holds no name and no free text; (2) one patient record-access row is written through the patient module's port (ids only; OQ-34-19); (3) a platform audit event `IPD_EMERGENCY_ACCESS_OPENED` is written with grant id, admission id, reason key and end time. If any of them fails, no grant exists and the user sees an error. Sheets opened during the grant write the rows they write today. The entry appears in the Clinical audit trail with the key icon (OWNER and DOCTOR: all entries; NURSE: her own). (Rule 14)
59. Given a grant, then neither the screen nor the API can edit, shorten, back-date, delete or re-assign it; only the review fields are set later and only once (story 34F). (Rule 11, 13)
60. Given IPD is Trial, On or Read-only, then a grant can be opened (a patient already in the hospital must still be reachable by the ward; OQ-34-29). Given IPD Off, then 403 `module-off`. (Rule 16)
61. Given a slow or failed network on the request, then the sheet stays open with the choices kept, shows the server's message or "We could not open emergency access. Try again.", and does not claim access was granted; nothing is queued offline. (Rule 11, 12)
62. Given another clinic's patient or user, then it is never listed, never granted, never visible in any grant (RLS; the clinic comes from the signed-in user). (Rule 15)
63. Given a keyboard or screen-reader user, then every field has a visible label, the checkbox is labelled with its full sentence, errors are announced and keep focus on the field, focus returns to the Emergency access button when the sheet closes, buttons are at least 44 px on a tablet, and the card flag has the accessible text "Emergency access". (Rule 10)

Notes for design
- The mockup stores one grant per patient (`a.bg`); the data must hold one row per grant (ADR-016 D-2) so that the first grant is never lost from review.
- Times come from the database clock; the 60-second reload is the only way the list notices expiry between actions.
- The three reasons are a fixed list of keys; the texts in the audit entry and in the owner's panel are the same texts.

### SAH-34F · Owner review of emergency access

64. Given the Safety board and the user is OWNER, then a tile **Emergency access** shows N (grants not yet reviewed) with the small text "Waiting for your review", amber when N > 0, as the fourth tile where the mockup places it. Given DOCTOR, NURSE and every other role, then the tile and the panel are not shown and the server does not send them. (Rule 13)
65. Given N > 0, then below the Medicine round and above the Clinical audit trail a panel **Emergency access to review** (key icon) shows the subtitle "Staff opened patients outside their wards. Check each reason." and one row per unreviewed grant. Given N = 0, then the panel is not shown (as the mockup). (Rule 13)
66. Given a row, then it reads "<staff name> opened <patient name> · bed <code>", with the small line "<reason> · <time> · outside their wards" (the time as "11:05 AM" today, "24 Oct, 11:05 AM" earlier), and the button **Mark reviewed**. The staff name and patient name are read at display time ("Team member" if the user is unknown); the bed code is the bed at the time of the grant, not the bed now. Two grants on one patient are two rows. (Rule 13, 14; OQ-34-34)
67. Given an unreviewed grant, then it stays in the panel after it expired, after the patient was discharged, and after the staff member became inactive, until it is reviewed. This differs from the mockup (it lists admitted patients only) and is accepted (DEC-031). (Rule 13)
68. Given **Mark reviewed**, when the owner taps it, then the server records the owner and the time once, the row leaves the panel, N drops by one, and the toast reads "Marked as reviewed". The button is disabled while the request runs. (Rule 13)
69. Given the review, then it is OWNER only and never by the person who opened the grant; a second review of the same grant (two owners, a double tap) is refused with 409 `already-reviewed` and a notice "Already reviewed.", the panel reloads, and nothing is changed or written; the review fields can never be cleared or changed afterwards. Given DOCTOR, NURSE or another role, then the API answers 403 `forbidden`; given a grant of another clinic or an unknown id, 404. (Rule 13, 15)
70. Given a review, then a clinical audit entry of kind `bg` is written with the fixed text "Reviewed emergency access" (user = the owner, admission set), which holds no staff or patient name (the mockup's "… by <staff name>" is not used, because names are read at display time and never stored in an entry), and a platform audit event `IPD_EMERGENCY_ACCESS_REVIEWED` with grant id and admission id. The trail shows who opened the grant by reading the grant at display time. (Rule 14; OQ-34-20)
71. Given the owner's Safety tab, then its number includes the unreviewed grants, so that the owner sees something waiting without opening the tab (phase 1 has no notification); the tile, the panel rows and this part of the tab number are the same figure (OQ-34-30, a deviation from the mockup's count). (Rule 12, 13)
72. Given the panel is empty and the owner has never had a grant, then the tile reads 0 and is not amber; given the panel holds more than 25 rows, then the oldest unreviewed come first, 25 are shown, and a button **Show 25 more** adds the next 25 (OQ-34-32). (Rule 13)
73. Given IPD is Read-only, then the owner can still read the panel and Mark reviewed (it records oversight). Given IPD Off, 403 `module-off`. (Rule 16; OQ-34-29)
74. Given the 60-second reload of the board and a slow or failed network, then the panel follows the server; a failed Mark reviewed keeps the row, shows the error, and does not claim it was reviewed; a failed reload keeps the last data with "Could not refresh. Showing data from <time>." (Rule 13)
75. Given another clinic, then its grants are never listed or reviewed (RLS). (Rule 15)
76. Given privacy, then the panel is read at display time and the names are never written to a log or an audit entry; reading the board writes no record-access row (DEC-015); the reason is one of the three fixed texts (no free text can appear). (Rule 14, 15)
77. Given the mockup, then the following are not built because the mockup shows none: a comment or a "not OK" outcome on a review, a list of reviewed grants, a filter or date range, a second reviewer, an alert when the owner does not review, export. A clinic whose owner never reviews gets no technical warning other than the amber tile (OQ-34-31). (Rule 12, 13)

Notes for design
- The panel rows, the tile and the tab number come from one count of unreviewed grants, read in the same transaction as the rest of the board (no separate poll).
- The review is a single write of two fields on the grant row, guarded once; the architect decides how the 409 is detected.

78. **(Added 10 Nov 2026 after review; story 34E; picker audit.)** Given a successful `GET /emergency-access/patients` by a scoped NURSE (including a list of 0 patients), then exactly one platform audit event `IPD_EMERGENCY_ACCESS_PICKER_VIEWED` is written with the caller's user id as actor, no entity id, and only the number of patients returned; it holds no admission id, patient id, name, UHID or bed code. The event is written in the same transaction as the read. Given the audit write fails, then the request answers 500 and no list is returned. Given a refused request (401, 403 `forbidden`, 403 `module-off`), then no event is written. The picker still writes no record-access row and no clinical event (AC 57), and is allowed when IPD is Read-only (AC 60). (Rule 14, 15)
79. **(Added 10 Nov 2026 after review; story 34F; owner reload hint.)** Given at least one unreviewed emergency access in the clinic, then the OWNER's Safety change token carries one extra line `bg:<count>:<newest unreviewed grant id>`, and no such line when none is unreviewed. Given the owner has the Safety board or the Admitted list open, when a grant is opened or a grant is marked reviewed, then at the next 60-second reload (AC 40, AC 74) the token differs and the screens reload their data (tile, panel, tab number). Given DOCTOR, NURSE and every other role, then their token never carries a `bg` line and is unchanged by this story. The token holds no name and no patient data, and it is only compared for equality by the screens. A rare case, accepted in the design: two grants committed out of order, together with a review in the same poll interval, can leave the token unchanged once; the board itself is always read fresh. (Rule 12, 13)
80. **(Added 10 Nov 2026 after review; story 34B; discharged admissions.)** Given a DISCHARGED admission and a scoped NURSE with the switch on, then she can read it (`GET /admissions/{id}`, `/nursing`, `/emr`, `/mar`, `/discharge`) if and only if a live ward of hers is the ward of the admission's last bed; a nurse of any other ward gets 403 `outside-scope`. Given an emergency access, then it never gives access to a discharged admission: a grant counts only while the admission is admitted, so a nurse who reached the patient only through a grant loses the record at discharge, even within the 30 minutes (this refines AC 55; the grant still stays in the owner's list). Given a write to a discharged admission, then it is refused by the endpoint's own state check (`not-admitted`) after the scope check. This is narrower than before SAH-34, when every nurse could read every discharged admission. (Rule 3, 5, 11, 13)
81. **(Added 10 Nov 2026 after review; story 34B; clinical events, nothing in scope.)** Given a scoped NURSE with no live ward and no active emergency access, when she opens the Clinical audit trail (`GET /clinical-events`), then she sees only the entries she wrote herself (her user is the actor), in the same order and paging as for any other nurse, and no entry of any other user, whether the admission is discharged, outside her scope or has an emergency access entry of someone else. Given she has a ward or an active grant, then AC 11 applies (own entries plus entries of admissions in scope). The rows, their order and the paging are exactly those AC 11 would give for a nurse with nothing in scope; only the way they are read is cheaper (design 1.7). Given no entries of her own, then the page is empty. (Rule 4, 7)

New questions raised by the business analyst:

- **OQ-34-26 (SAFETY, needs human and the clinical sign-off OQ-30-6). The covering-nurse risk.** Hiding a patient from a covering nurse can hide a deteriorating patient. The ADR relies on Emergency access, on DOCTOR and OWNER being unscoped, and on the switch being off by default. A scoped nurse's board would otherwise say "All within target" about her wards only. **Recommended (A):** the board and list say they are scoped (AC 37, 36), OWNER and DOCTOR are never narrowed (AC 18), and the scoped nurse also sees the count, without names, of escalations past target in other wards (AC 20, AC 37). Alternative B: the ADR as written, scoped counts only, no other-ward count (remove AC 20 and its part of AC 37). Alternative C: do not enable scoping until a notification channel exists. Not covered in either option: overdue doses in other wards (the ward's own nurse gives them). The clinical sign-off owner must read ADR-016 section 8 before any clinic switches the scope on. The count is a BA addition: it is in neither the mockup nor the ADR.
